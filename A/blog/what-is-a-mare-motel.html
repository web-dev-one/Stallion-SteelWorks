What Is a Mare Motel?

A mare motel is a steel shade structure combined with individual or grouped pipe pens, designed to give horses a shaded, open-air holding and turnout space. Despite the name, mare motels are used for mares, geldings, stallions, young horses, and mixed-use facilities.

Simple definition: A mare motel is an open-sided, steel-roofed structure with pipe pens underneath that provides shade, airflow, and efficient daily horse management.

Why horse owners use mare motels

Mare motels solve a common problem: horses need shade and structure without the downsides of enclosed barns. In hot climates, traditional stalls trap heat, limit airflow, and require more labor to keep clean.

  • Shade without heat buildup – open sides allow constant airflow.
  • Lower construction cost than full barns.
  • Faster cleaning compared to enclosed stalls.
  • Flexible use – turnout, short-term holding, feeding, breeding, or boarding.
  • Expandable – pens, gates, or utility rooms can be added later.

Mare motel vs traditional horse stalls

Mare motel advantages:
  • Natural airflow reduces heat stress.
  • No enclosed walls trapping ammonia or heat.
  • Lower maintenance and fewer repairs.
  • Horses can see surroundings, reducing stress.
Traditional stall advantages:
  • Weather protection in cold or extreme storms.
  • More control for medical confinement.

Many Arizona facilities choose mare motels for daily use and reserve enclosed stalls for special cases.

Common mare motel layouts

Mare motels are highly customizable. Layout depends on how horses are moved, cleaned, and worked.

Popular configurations

  • Single-row motel: pens along one side of a shade roof.
  • Double-row motel: pens on both sides with a center work lane.
  • U-shaped motel: pens wrapped around a work or wash area.
  • Integrated utility: tack room, hay storage, or wash rack under the same roof.

Ventilation: why mare motels stay cooler

Mare motels rely on open-air design. Instead of fighting heat, they allow it to escape. Steel roofs block direct sun while open sides let hot air rise and move away.

  • Higher roof clearance improves air movement.
  • Open sides prevent heat trapping.
  • Optional partial walls block low-angle sun without stopping airflow.

Safety and panel design

Good mare motel design accounts for horse behavior—leaning, rubbing, pawing, and crowding. Pipe panels are chosen for strength, spacing consistency, and smooth finishes.

  • Consistent rail spacing prevents legs or heads getting caught.
  • Heavy gate and corner posts handle pressure points.
  • Horse-safe latches reduce injury risk.

Cleaning and daily efficiency

One of the biggest reasons owners prefer mare motels is cleaning speed. Open layouts allow wheelbarrows and equipment to move freely without tight turns.

  • Wide access lanes for equipment.
  • Grade and pad prep prevent mud and standing water.
  • Roof runoff managed away from pen entrances.
Reality: If a mare motel is easy to clean, it stays cleaner—and horses stay healthier.

Who mare motels are best for

  • Arizona and Southwest horse owners
  • Boarding facilities
  • Breeding operations
  • Training barns
  • Ranch and private horse properties

Mare motels are especially effective where heat, sun exposure, and daily labor efficiency matter.
